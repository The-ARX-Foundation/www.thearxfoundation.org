§ 01The Caravan Hypothesis

Core assertion: Current civilization is not "car-centric" — it's home-centric architecture where cars function as mobile home extensions, not transportation. The suburbs are a low-density distributed housing model where each unit happens to move.

What if we accept this? What if we stop pretending cars are for going places and acknowledge they're sovereign, climate-controlled private capsules that people pilot between stationary life-support modules (house → office → store)?

The Caravan is a civilization redesign that makes mobility the primary architectural assumption. Not "people who sometimes move" — people who are always moving, always in formation, always reconfiguring.

§ 01.1What Changes

If we design for mobility-as-default rather than residence-as-default:

  • No property ownership. Territory is temporary. Parking becomes "anchorage" — negotiated access to power/water/data, not real estate.
  • Decentralized services. No centralized water/sewage/power grids. Every unit is semi-autonomous (solar, battery, water reclamation). Infrastructure is peer-to-peer.
  • Dynamic social graphs. Your neighbors change weekly. Relationships form around proximity duration and need compatibility, not geographic permanence.
  • Needs become tradeable signals. "I need a shower" becomes a broadcast. "I have extra water capacity" becomes an offer. Value flows through the mesh.
The Caravan treats geographic stasis as the anomaly, not movement. Stability comes from protocol, not position.
§ 02Mobile Unit Spec

Every Caravan unit is a self-contained life-support module with the following baseline:

Autonomy Stack

  • Power: 800W+ solar, 300Ah+ LiFePO4, shore power adapter (universal plug standard)
  • Water: 40gal freshwater, 30gal greywater, portable black tank exchange system
  • Climate: 12V roof AC, diesel/propane heater, insulation rated to -10°C / 40°C exterior
  • Data: Starlink or equivalent (500Mbps min), mesh radio (LoRa/Meshtastic backup layer), local NAS
  • Propulsion: 300+ mile range, 4WD/AWD for dirt roads, <25ft length for maneuverability
§ 02.1Shared Infrastructure Nodes

Permanent infrastructure exists, but it's service-oriented, not residence-oriented. Think truck stops, not suburbs.

Node Type Services Provided Capacity
Anchorage Hub 50A shore power, potable water fill, blackwater dump, laundry, showers 50-200 units
Maker Space CNC, 3D printing, welding, electronics bench, tool library 10-30 concurrent
Health Clinic General practice, dental, pharmacy, telemedicine relay Mobile, 5-10/day
Provisioning Depot Bulk food (co-op model), propane, diesel, repair parts 100+ units/day
Knowledge Exchange Library, skill workshops, server rack (data backup/sync) 20-50 concurrent

These nodes are spaced 50-150 miles apart along major routes. No one lives at a node — they're visited, used, and departed.

Infrastructure serves flow, not fixity. The goal is friction-free access to services, not attachment to place.
§ 03Coordination Protocols

Without fixed addresses, fixed neighbors, or fixed schedules, protocol becomes the only stable substrate. These are the social/technical agreements that make Caravan life viable.

Mesh Broadcast Standard (MBS)

Every unit runs a low-power LoRa transceiver that broadcasts:

  • Position: GPS coords updated every 5 min (opt-in for privacy, but most broadcast)
  • Needs: Current resource state (water: 60%, power: 80%, propane: 40%)
  • Offers: Surplus capacity available for trade/gift
  • Skills: What you can do (mechanic, medic, chef, welder, teacher)
  • Availability: Open to visitors, working (DND), emergency

Range: ~10 miles in open terrain. Forms ad-hoc mesh when 3+ units are in range.

Caravan Formation Rules

When multiple units travel together ("a caravan"), coordination defaults:

  • Lead rotation: Every 2 hours, leadership rotates (prevents burnout, distributes navigation labor)
  • Spacing: 100-200ft between units (safety + radio mesh integrity)
  • Break sync: Majority vote for fuel/food/rest stops (broadcast via mesh)
  • Emergency protocol: Any unit flashes hazards 3x = full stop, render aid
  • Departure opt-out: If you're not ready when caravan moves, you split off (no guilt, no penalty)

Needs Routing Algorithm

Units broadcast needs. Other units auto-respond if they can fulfill. Example:

  • Unit A broadcasts: NEED: shower, range=5mi, willing_to_pay=true
  • Unit B responds: OFFER: shower, fee=10min_labor OR $5, available_now=true
  • Unit A accepts: ACCEPT: labor, arrive_15min
  • Exchange occurs. Both units log transaction (reputation system).

Payment can be: cash, labor, barter, gift. Protocol is agnostic. Reputation is the true currency.

§ 03.1Dispute Resolution

No police. No courts. Conflicts resolved through:

  • Mesh arbitration: Nearby units volunteer as mediators. Majority vote binds both parties.
  • Reputation downvote: Bad actors get flagged. After 3 flags, most caravans refuse formation with you.
  • Physical distance: If mediation fails, parties separate. No one is forced to stay near anyone.

Violence = immediate mesh-wide ban. Permanent exile from protocol networks.

Governance is emergent, not hierarchical. Authority comes from proximity + participation, not titles or territory.
§ 04Decentralized Needs Allocation

In a static society, needs are met through stores (centralized inventory) and jobs (centralized labor). In The Caravan, needs are met through peer-to-peer matching — a living, breathing market of surplus and deficit.

§ 04.1Resource Categories

Consumables: Water, food, fuel, propane. Metered, traded, gifted. Units with surplus broadcast availability. Units in need request.

Services: Repair, medical, teaching, child care, cooking. Skill-holders broadcast hours available. Requesters broadcast need + proposed compensation.

Space: Shelter (guest bed), storage (use my cargo box), workspace (borrow my desk). Temporary access, not ownership.

Knowledge: Tutorials, troubleshooting, emotional support. Freely given, reputation-tracked.

§ 04.2Example: Water Reallocation

Unit A is at 15% freshwater. Unit B is at 85% and anchored near a stream with filtration gear.

  1. A broadcasts: NEED: water, 20gal, urgency=medium, location=GPS
  2. B auto-responds: OFFER: water, 20gal, fee=none, note="refilling_at_stream"
  3. A accepts, drives to B. B fills A's tank. A offers to split propane costs for B's water pump. B accepts.
  4. Both log positive interaction. Reputation +1 for both parties.

Alternative: If no nearby units have surplus, A's request escalates to "urgency=high" and broadcasts to wider mesh. Someone 20 miles away might drive water to A for a fee.

§ 04.3Example: Skill Bartering

Unit C's refrigerator dies. Unit D is a refrigeration tech but needs help with child care.

  1. C broadcasts: NEED: fridge_repair, skills_available=[cooking, guitar], payment=[labor OR $50]
  2. D responds: OFFER: fridge_repair, fee=4hrs_childcare, arrive_tonight=true
  3. C accepts. D fixes fridge (~2 hrs work). C watches D's kids for 4 hrs the next day.
  4. Exchange complete. Both parties satisfied. Reputation +1.
§ 04.4Reputation as Currency

Cash still exists, but reputation is the real wealth. High-reputation units get:

  • Priority responses to needs broadcasts
  • Invitations to exclusive caravans (skilled travelers only)
  • Discounts or free services from grateful past recipients
  • Dispute mediation weighted in their favor

Low-reputation units (flags for theft, violence, scamming) get frozen out. No one wants to form caravans with them. No one responds to their needs. Social death precedes physical isolation.

The Needs Mesh is self-healing — as units move, new pairings form. Scarcity in one location gets rebalanced by units arriving from elsewhere. The system breathes.
§ 05What Breaks, What Emerges

What dies:

  • Property tax (no property). Sales tax (peer trades bypass retail). Income tax (labor is barter).
  • Zoning laws. Building codes. HOAs. All premised on fixed structures.
  • Public schools (replaced by itinerant tutors + mesh knowledge bases).
  • Hospitals (replaced by mobile clinics + telemedicine).
  • Mortgages, landlords, rent. The entire real estate extraction economy.
  • Commuting (you live where you work, which is wherever you park).

What emerges:

  • Skill economy: People become valuable for what they know and do, not what they own.
  • Ephemeral community: Deep, intense friendships formed over weeks, then dissolved as people move. Relationships valued for their duration, not permanence.
  • Ecological flexibility: People migrate seasonally (follow good weather, avoid disasters). Climate change becomes a navigation problem, not a property destruction problem.
  • Information commons: Every unit syncs knowledge bases at hubs. Wikis, repair manuals, maps, cultural archives — all open, all replicated.
  • Children raised collectively: Rotating caregivers. Kids grow up knowing 200 adults intimately instead of 5. Abuse harder to hide (too many eyes).
§ 05.1Who This Serves, Who It Doesn't

Thrives: Builders, fixers, teachers, artists, caregivers. People who produce value through labor/skill. Introverts (you can always drive away). Neurodivergent folks (design your own routine, no boss).

Struggles: People who need intensive medical infrastructure (dialysis, chemotherapy). Elderly with limited mobility. People psychologically dependent on geographic permanence. Capitalists (can't extract rent when everyone's mobile).

Note: Medical pods (mobile ICU units) could address chronic care. Elderly-only caravans form naturally. But The Caravan is not a utopia — it optimizes for different values (mobility, autonomy, direct reciprocity) and some people won't fit.

§ 05.2Threat Models

State violence: Governments that rely on property tax + captive labor pools will resist. Expect laws criminalizing van dwelling, mesh networks, unlicensed education. Caravan members become de facto outlaws.

Bandit caravans: Armed groups could raid isolated units. Defense: travel in numbers, mesh early warning systems, avoid low-trust zones.

Resource hoarding: Units stockpiling water/fuel instead of sharing. Mitigation: reputation system makes hoarders pariahs. Generosity is survival strategy.

Mesh breakdown: If radios fail, protocol collapses. Backup: visual signals (flags), analog maps, pre-agreed meeting points.

§ 05.3Bootstrapping the Caravan

You can't force a civilization redesign. But you can prototype:

  1. Build the mesh. Open-source LoRa firmware. Get 100 units running it.
  2. Establish first hubs. Negotiate with truck stops, BLM land, cooperative farms. 5-10 anchor points along a 500-mile corridor.
  3. Document everything. What worked. What broke. What hurt. Make the knowledge replicable.
  4. Invite the desperate. Van lifers. Homeless. Refugees. People already mobile by necessity. Give them protocol, tools, community.
  5. Let it evolve. Don't force the design. Watch what emerges. Codify the patterns that survive.
The Caravan isn't a blueprint. It's a design space — a set of constraints (mobility, decentralization, peer reciprocity) that generates emergent order. We don't know what it looks like at scale. We build it to find out.
