The Fuel Intelligence Gap

Every diesel driver in the United States operates blind. They know price — maybe, if GasBuddy's crowd-sourced number is recent. They do not know what is in the fuel. Not the biodiesel blend percentage. Not the additive package. Not whether the pump they're standing at is feeding their high-pressure common rail engine a chemistry that will quietly destroy it from the inside.

The 2019 Mercedes Sprinter VS30 — and every modern HPCR diesel — operates with injector tolerances measured in microns, at pressures exceeding 30,000 PSI. A B-20 biodiesel fill that Mercedes explicitly warns against can accelerate injector deposit formation, dilute engine oil, clog fuel filters, and reduce engine output. But the only way to know you're pumping B-20 is to read a sticker on the pump face. No app tracks this. No service reports it. The information exists — printed on a 2×3 inch label — and dies there.

The most critical data about what enters your engine is the least digitized data in the entire fuel supply chain. FuelMesh exists to close that gap — not by asking corporations for API access, but by building a decentralized observation network that reads the world directly.

FuelMesh is an open-source, crowd-sourced fuel intelligence protocol. It combines mobile OCR capture (pump photos), passive dashcam sign reading, GPS-tagged chemistry logging, and mesh synchronization to build the first fuel database that tracks not just price but composition — the actual molecular profile of what's going into your engine, at every station, in real time.

Origin incident — April 8, 2026: A fuel stop in The Colony, TX. AI-sourced price data hallucinated $3.09/gal. Actual pump price: $5.499/gal. Pump labeled B-20 biodiesel — a blend Mercedes recommends against for regular use. 11.128 gallons entered the tank before the chemistry was identified. Total cost: $61.19 for fuel the engine shouldn't be running on. FuelMesh was born from this failure.
What's Broken
Price data is centralized and stale. GasBuddy, Google, AAA — all depend on corporate data feeds or delayed crowd-sourcing with no verification layer. Diesel prices are especially volatile and underreported.
Fuel chemistry is invisible. No consumer-facing service tracks biodiesel blend ratios, additive packages, or ULSD compliance at the station level. This data exists on pump labels and is never digitized.
Engine-specific risk is unmodeled. A B-20 fill is fine for an old mechanical diesel. It's a maintenance liability for a 2019 HPCR Sprinter. No service correlates fuel chemistry with engine compatibility.
Verification is impossible. When a service says diesel is $3.09 and you arrive to $5.49, there is no recourse, no correction mechanism, no accountability loop.

System Architecture

FuelMesh operates on two data ingestion layers — active capture (user-initiated pump photos) and passive capture (dashcam OCR on roadside price signs) — feeding into a shared mesh that synchronizes via delay-tolerant networking. No central server required for core operation. The network functions offline-first, syncing when connectivity is available.

No APIs. No corporate permission. No rate limits. The observation network reads the physical world directly — the same way a human would, but at scale.
Active Capture Layer
Primary Data Source
User photographs fuel pump at fill-up. Mobile OCR pipeline extracts: price per gallon (from display), fuel type/blend (from pump label sticker — B-5, B-20, ULSD, R99, etc.), station identity (from pump branding or GPS lookup), and total gallons/cost (from receipt display). GPS coordinates and timestamp are embedded automatically. One photo per fill generates a complete fuel intelligence record.
PIPELINE: Camera → On-device OCR (Tesseract/PaddleOCR) → Schema validation → Local DB → Mesh sync
Passive Capture Layer
Ambient Intelligence
Dashcam-mounted continuous OCR reads roadside fuel price signs while driving. Character recognition on high-contrast price displays (large numerals, standardized formats) is a significantly easier target than license plate reading — which is already a solved problem at highway speed. A Raspberry Pi Zero 2W + camera module running PaddleOCR can process frames in real-time, logging GPS-stamped price observations without user interaction.
HARDWARE: RPi Zero 2W + Camera Module 3 (~$35) · INFERENCE: PaddleOCR-lite · STORAGE: SQLite → Mesh sync via Tailscale/BLE
Mesh Synchronization
Core Protocol
FuelMesh nodes sync via delay-tolerant networking — the same paradigm as iOverlander, Briar, or Scuttlebutt. Nodes that encounter each other (BLE proximity, shared WiFi, Tailscale mesh) exchange observations using CRDTs (Conflict-free Replicated Data Types) to merge without coordination. Internet connectivity enables wider sync through lightweight relay servers, but is never required for local mesh operation.
SYNC: CRDTs (Automerge/Yjs) · TRANSPORT: BLE mesh + Tailscale + optional relay · CONFLICT: Last-write-wins per station-timestamp pair
Verification Engine
Trust Layer
Multi-observation convergence scoring. A single user's price report has low confidence. Three independent observations within 24 hours at the same station GPS cluster converge to high confidence. Photo evidence (raw OCR source image) is retained as proof-of-observation, hashed and timestamped. Sybil resistance through spatial uniqueness — hard to fake GPS + OCR + photo + timestamp coherence.
TRUST: Convergence scoring · PROOF: SHA-256 hashed source images · SYBIL: GPS + temporal + OCR coherence check
Station Node Hardware
Physical Infrastructure
Open-hardware physical feedback nodes deployed at fuel stations — inspired by FeedbackNow's smiley-button form factor (Forrester/Nasdaq: FORR, 200K+ daily interactions across 500+ orgs) but decentralized and open-data. Three-button interface for amenity rating (restroom, pump condition, overall). Optional digital display reader for real-time price capture. Fuel chemistry set once per delivery batch by station staff (in exchange for foot traffic data from the mesh). Battery-powered, BLE beacon — syncs to any FuelMesh device that drives past. No WiFi, no cloud dependency. The anti-FeedbackNow: data goes to the public mesh, not a corporate SaaS silo.
HARDWARE: ESP32-S3 + BLE 5.0 + e-ink display (~$12 BOM) · POWER: CR123A lithium (2+ year life) · SYNC: BLE passive broadcast
OpenStreetMap Integration
Upstream Contribution
FuelMesh observations map directly onto OSM's existing amenity=fuel schema with extended tags: fuel:biodiesel_blend=B20, fuel:diesel:price=5.499, fuel:diesel:price:date=2026-04-08, restroom=yes, restroom:fee=no, restroom:cleanliness=good|fair|poor, fuel:diesel:chemistry_verified=yes. OSM already has the station nodes — it just has no chemistry layer and no amenity freshness. FuelMesh fills that gap and contributes upstream. Every observation enriches the commons.
FORMAT: OSM XML/PBF changeset export · TAGS: Extended fuel:* + amenity:* namespace · LICENSE: ODbL (OSM-compatible)
Distributed Verification
Trust Without Extraction
Verification is built into the contribution protocol itself — every user who confirms an existing observation at the same station is performing verification work. No separate labor marketplace needed. The iOverlander model proves this: users ARE the verifiers. For OCR ground-truth training data (bootstrapping the model), ethical micro-task platforms like Hive or Toloka can supplement — but never Amazon MTurk. The labor market for FuelMesh verification must match the ethics of the protocol: transparent pay, no data extraction, contributors own their work product.
MODEL: User-as-verifier (primary) · BOOTSTRAP: Ethical micro-task (Hive/Toloka) for OCR training · NEVER: Amazon MTurk

Contribution Protocol
Snap & Submit: Photograph the pump face at any diesel fill. OCR extracts all visible data. User confirms/corrects. One tap to submit to local mesh.
Passive Drive: Mount dashcam with FuelMesh companion app or RPi unit. Price signs are read automatically, GPS-tagged, and queued for mesh sync. Zero user interaction after initial setup.
Manual Entry: For stations without photo opportunity — log price, chemistry, and station manually. Lower confidence score but still contributes to the mesh.
Verification Bounty: Users who confirm an existing observation (second independent photo at same station within 24h) earn elevated trust score. Convergence drives confidence.
OSM Upstream Export

Every FuelMesh observation enriches OpenStreetMap. The protocol maps directly onto OSM's existing amenity=fuel node schema with extended properties in the fuel:* namespace. Observations are batched into OSM changesets and contributed upstream under ODbL — the same license OSM uses. This is public infrastructure data contributed to the public commons. Not a walled garden. Not a SaaS dashboard. A public utility.

Amenity & Cleanliness Layer

Fuel stations are not just fuel dispensers — they're infrastructure nodes for anyone living on the road. Van lifers, truckers, overlanders, and traveling workers need restrooms, air pumps, water, DEF, dump stations, and safe parking. This data is scattered across iOverlander, Trucker Path, AllStays, and a dozen other proprietary apps that don't talk to each other. FuelMesh unifies it into a single observation that covers chemistry, price, AND amenities — then pushes it all to OSM where it benefits everyone, not just app users.

Restroom: Available (yes/no), cleanliness rating (good/fair/poor via 3-button input or app), fee required, accessibility notes.
Air & Water: Free air pump, potable water fill station — critical for van/RV life.
DEF at Pump: Whether diesel exhaust fluid is dispensed at the pump island vs. only sold in bottles inside.
Dump Station: RV/van dump availability — one of the most underreported pieces of road infrastructure.
Parking: Truck/van overnight viability — safe, lit, tolerated, ticketed, or explicitly prohibited. Time-stamped observations from actual overnighters.

Development Roadmap

FuelMesh is designed to ship value from day one. Phase 0 is the fuel log that already exists — the personal vehicle log built tonight for the Sprinter "Flux." Each phase adds a layer of network effect, moving from single-user utility to multi-user intelligence to ambient passive collection.

Phase 0 — Now
Personal Fuel Log
Single-user fuel chemistry and maintenance tracker. Manual entry with rich schema: fuel type, blend %, price, station, additives, odometer, DTC codes. Already built and operational for Flux (VS30 Sprinter). Persistent storage across sessions. This is the seed — every FuelMesh user starts here.
Phase 1 — MVP
Pump Photo OCR
Mobile app (PWA or native) with camera integration. Point at pump → OCR extracts price, blend label, pump number. GPS auto-tags location. User confirms extracted data. Stores locally in SQLite. Export to JSON/CSV. No network required — works completely offline. Target: functional on GrapheneOS Pixel 9.
Phase 2 — Network
Mesh Sync + Station Map + OSM Export
Local-first CRDT sync between FuelMesh users. When two devices encounter each other (BLE, shared Tailscale network, WiFi direct), observations merge automatically. Map layer (MapLibre + Protomaps) shows all known stations with last-observed price and chemistry. Station clustering by GPS coordinate proximity. Confidence scoring based on observation recency and convergence. Automated OSM changeset generation — every verified observation enriches OpenStreetMap's amenity=fuel nodes with chemistry, price, and amenity tags under ODbL.
Phase 3 — Passive
Dashcam OCR + Price Sign Reading
Raspberry Pi Zero 2W + Camera Module 3 companion unit. Mounts to windshield or dash. Runs PaddleOCR-lite continuously while driving. Recognizes fuel price signs (high-contrast, standardized font, large numerals) and logs GPS-stamped observations. Syncs to phone via BLE. No user interaction after initial mount — pure ambient intelligence. Stretch: integrate with existing dashcam hardware (Viofo, BlackVue) via RTSP stream.
Phase 4 — Physical Nodes
Station Hardware + Amenity Layer
Open-hardware ESP32-S3 feedback nodes deployed at fuel stations. Three-button amenity rating (restroom cleanliness, pump condition, overall experience). Optional price display reader for continuous capture. BLE beacon broadcasts station state to any passing FuelMesh device. Amenity schema tracks restrooms, air pumps, water fill, DEF at pump, dump stations, overnight parking tolerance — the full road-life infrastructure layer that iOverlander, Trucker Path, and AllStays each capture partially but none unify. Station operators opt in via a value exchange: foot traffic analytics from the mesh in return for chemistry data per delivery batch.
Phase 5 — Intelligence
Route Optimization + Alerts
With sufficient mesh density: predictive pricing (time-of-day, day-of-week patterns), route-optimized fueling recommendations ("fill at Forney, skip Terrell — $0.40/gal savings, ULSD confirmed"), and real-time chemistry alerts ("station at your next exit is B-20 — your engine profile flags this"). Integration with vehicle OBD-II for automatic odometer and DTC sync.
Phase 6 — Protocol
Open Standard + Federation
FuelMesh observation format published as open specification (CC BY-SA 4.0). Third-party apps can read/write to the mesh. Federation between regional FuelMesh instances. Potential integration with fleet management systems, truck stop networks, and DOT fuel quality monitoring programs. The protocol becomes infrastructure — like GPS, not like Google Maps.

The FuelMesh Thesis

The fuel supply chain generates billions of dollars in revenue from information asymmetry. The driver at the pump is the only participant in the system who doesn't know what they're buying. FuelMesh inverts that.

Observation is a sovereign act. When you photograph a fuel pump, you are not "contributing data to a platform." You are exercising your right to observe and record the physical world around you. FuelMesh does not own your observations. You do. The mesh carries them because you choose to share. You can stop at any time. Your device, your camera, your data.

Chemistry matters more than price. The entire fuel app ecosystem optimizes for one variable: cents per gallon. FuelMesh recognizes that the composition of the fuel — blend ratio, additive package, sulfur content, cetane rating — has a larger impact on total cost of ownership than price delta. A $0.20/gal savings on B-20 that accelerates your $3,000 injector replacement is not a savings. It's a trap.

Decentralization is not an ideology. It's an engineering requirement. Fuel prices change hourly. Pump chemistry varies by delivery batch. Corporate APIs update daily at best. The only system capable of tracking ground truth at the pace it changes is a distributed observation network with contributors at every station. Central servers can aggregate and relay, but the observation layer must be peer-to-peer or it will always be stale.

The protocol must outlive the project. FuelMesh is licensed AGPL-3.0. The observation schema is published CC BY-SA 4.0. If ARX Foundation disappears tomorrow, the data format and sync protocol remain. Anyone can build a FuelMesh-compatible client. Anyone can run a relay. The network is the product — not the app, not the server, not the organization.

Every diesel driver who has been burned by bad data, surprise chemistry, or opaque pricing is already a FuelMesh node. They just don't have the protocol yet.
