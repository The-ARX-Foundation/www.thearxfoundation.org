Theban Alphabet

The Honorian Script / Witches' Alphabet

Origin: Trithemius, Polygraphia (1518)
Characters: 24
Type: Latin substitution cipher
Direction: Left-to-right
Status: Actively used in modern practice

Historical Context

Attributed to the fictional "Honorius of Thebes" in Trithemius's cryptographic manual Polygraphia. Despite dubious origins, it became foundational to Western ceremonial magic after being reprinted by Agrippa in Three Books of Occult Philosophy (1533). Still widely used in contemporary Wicca and ceremonial magic for inscribing ritual tools, Books of Shadows, and sacred texts.

Glyphkeeper Practice Strategy: One-to-one Latin mapping makes this ideal for beginner cipher work. Practice by transcribing short English phrases, then progress to copying historical grimoire passages. The simplicity masks profound lineage — every glyph connects to 500+ years of Western occult tradition.

Steganographic Spirit Sigils

Angelic Cipher Keys from the Ars Steganographia

Origin: Trithemius, c. 1499–1500s
Sigils: 67+ distinct glyphs
Type: Cipher key identification system
Structure: Regional (32) + Wandering (11) + Temporal (24)

Cryptographic Theory

Each sigil identifies a specific encryption protocol hidden within the spirit's conjuration. The "angel magic" layer is a steganographic cover — the real message is encoded in the Latin invocations using variable skip patterns, alternating word extraction, and positional ciphers. Trithemius stressed perfect sigil copying not for magical reasons but to preserve cipher key integrity. A corrupted sigil = a corrupted decryption method.

Design Philosophy: Deliberately subtle glyphs meant to pass as medieval manuscript abbreviations or calligraphic flourishes to untrained eyes. Security through obscurity — the Inquisition wouldn't recognize these as magical unless they already knew what to look for.

Polygraphic Alchemical Cipher

Alchemical Symbols as Cryptographic Alphabet

Origin: Trithemius, Polygraphia (1518)
Symbols: ~30 core glyphs
Type: Substitution cipher using traditional alchemical notation
Cover: Appears as legitimate alchemical notes

Metaphysical Justification

Trithemius argued that manipulating symbolic language — whether Hebrew divine names, Pythagorean numerology, or alchemical glyphs — was spiritually transformative. Encryption itself became a path to divine illumination: "the soul can reach from earth to heaven" through linguistic manipulation. This fusion of semiotics and ontology made cryptography a sacred art, not merely a practical technique.

Historical Context: Created plausible deniability for political/military intelligence. An alchemical manuscript could conceal state secrets, military movements, or espionage reports while appearing to be harmless metallurgical research. If intercepted, it looked like gold-making delusions rather than treason.
